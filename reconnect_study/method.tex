\section{Selective track reactivation}

Let $x$ record the detector confidence, predicted box residuals, track
age and observed gap, and the costs of competing one-to-one
assignments when a tracker proposes reactivating an old track. Write
$m_r$ and $m_c$ for the best admissible competitor cost minus
the chosen cost in the same row or column. The model uses
$m=\min(m_r,m_c)$ to represent the side with the weaker margin.
We first apply base feature transformations $z(x)$, then form the
interaction vector and standardize its coordinates before fitting
the score
\begin{align*}
g(x) &= b+w^\top \phi(x),\\
\phi(x) &= [z(x),\,m,\,|m_r-m_c|,\,h d,\,h o,\,h m,\,q m,\ldots].
\end{align*}
Here $h$ is the transformed elapsed gap, $d$ and $o$ are the
normalized center difference and box overlap, and $q$ is detection
confidence. The interaction terms allow the weight of motion
agreement and assignment ambiguity to change with gap length.
We fit $g$ with regularized logistic loss on annotated development
proposals. An ablation adds a within-source ranking loss
$\log(1+\exp(g_{\rm incorrect}-g_{\rm correct}))$.
A threshold on $g$ governs reactivation. A rejected detection
returns to the tracker as unmatched, allowing the next association
step to reflect the new track state.

\subsection{A finite-panel risk bound}
We model outcomes at the source-video level. For each frozen policy
and source $s$, let $W_s$ count failed evaluated reactivations and
$A_s$ count evaluated accepted reactivations. The gate enforces an
action cap $B$, so $0\leq W_s\leq A_s\leq B$. The event-weighted
fixed-panel risk is $R=\sum_s W_s/\sum_s A_s$, defined when the
denominator is positive. For a target $r\in(0,1)$ and fixed
stake $t\in[0,1]$, define a nonnegative factor
\[
f_{s,t}(r) = 1+t\frac{r A_s - W_s}{B(1-r)}.
\]
If $R\geq r$, the arithmetic mean of $f_{s,t}(r)$ across the
panel is at most one. For a uniformly drawn $n$-source subset $I$,
the expected product $\operatorname{E}_I\prod_{s\in I}f_{s,t}(r)$ equals
a normalized elementary symmetric mean. Maclaurin's inequality bounds
it by the $n$th power of the arithmetic mean. The product therefore
has expectation at most one under the null. We use the fixed mixture
at $t=1/2$ and $t=1$ and invert one-sided tests obtained by Markov's
inequality. The same construction estimates the average per-video risk
across active videos by conditioning on the active sample count and
using fractional losses $W_s/A_s$ on those sources.
We allocate error probability across the two estimands and all frozen
threshold policies. The guarantee is taken over the random calibration
draw, conditional on source outcomes and frozen policies.

Learn Then Test formulates this choice of usable risks as multiple
testing \cite{angelopoulos2021learn}. Finite-population betting
procedures motivate operating on sampled sources
\cite{waudbysmith2020without}. Prior guaranteed tracking work controls
coverage of candidate transition sets \cite{li2022pac}, and MOT-CUP
propagates uncertainty about detector boxes \cite{su2024motcup}.
Here the loss is defined on the reactivations that the tracker commits
after closed-loop execution.
